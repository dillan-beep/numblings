# Curriculum Research: Skill Ladders, Starting Levels, Mastery Rules

Purpose: a concrete spec for question generators. Levels run 1–10 per domain. Level 1 ≈ late kindergarten / early grade 1; level 10 ≈ grade 6–7. Each level lists: concept, number range, example questions, misconception to watch for, best visual model.

Alignment: Ontario Mathematics Curriculum 2020 (strands B Number, C Algebra, D Data, E Spatial Sense, F Financial Literacy) and US Common Core (K–7). Where the two differ, Ontario leads because the family is in Ontario; Common Core differences are noted inline.

## 0. Grade anchors (what the two curricula expect per grade)

| Grade | Ontario 2020 Number range | Ontario fluency focus | Common Core required fluency |
|---|---|---|---|
| K | count to 30ish, subitize to 5 | compose/decompose to 10 | add/subtract within 5 |
| 1 | whole numbers to 50 (B1.1) | add/sub facts to 10 recalled, to 20 with strategies; skip count by 1s, 2s, 5s, 10s; coins to 50¢ | add/sub within 10 |
| 2 | to 200 | add/sub facts to 20 recalled; add/sub to 100; equal groups; coins to 200¢ | add/sub within 20 (facts), within 100 (paper) |
| 3 | to 1000 | multiplication facts for 2, 5, 10 (Ontario) ; add/sub to 1000; unit fractions; making change | multiply/divide within 100; add/sub within 1000 |
| 4 | to 10 000 | multiplication facts to 10 x 10 recalled; decimal tenths; fractions with like denominators | add/sub within 1 000 000; multi-digit x 1-digit |
| 5 | to 100 000 | facts to 12 x 12 recalled; decimal hundredths; percent introduced; integers introduced | multi-digit multiplication |
| 6 | to 1 000 000 | decimal thousandths; ratios/rates; integers on number line; order of operations | multi-digit division, decimal operations |
| 7 | rational numbers | integer operations; fraction x/÷; percents of quantities; exponents (squares/cubes) | solve px + q = r |

Note: Common Core puts multiplication facts fully in grade 3 (to 10 x 10 by end of year); Ontario spreads them 3–5 (2/5/10 in grade 3, to 10 x 10 in grade 4, to 12 x 12 in grade 5). The game should follow the faster Common Core pace, since the goal is to move above grade level.

## A. Skill ladders (10 levels per domain)

Format per level: **concept** | range | examples | misconception | model.

### A1. Counting and number sense

1. Count forward from 1, one-to-one, subitize dot patterns | 0–10 | "How many dots?" (ten frame with 7); "What comes after 6?" | Skipping or double-counting objects; saying the sequence without matching to objects | Ten frame, dot cards (dice patterns)
2. Count on from any number; count backward; ordinal to 10th | 0–20 | "Start at 13, count on 3"; "What comes before 17?" | Counting from 1 every time instead of counting on; teen-number reversals (12 vs 21) | Number line 0–20, ten frames (two)
3. Skip count by 2s, 5s, 10s; count to 50 | 0–50 | "5, 10, 15, __"; "Count by 2s from 12" | Treating skip counting as a chant rather than groups of the same size | Hundred chart (highlight columns), coins (nickels/dimes)
4. Count to 100/120 crossing decades; skip count from any start | 0–120 | "68, 69, __, 71"; "Count by 10s from 23" | Decade crossing errors ("29, 20-ten"); off-decade skip counting | Hundred chart, open number line
5. Count to 1000 by 1s, 10s, 100s across hundreds; even/odd | 0–1000 | "398, 399, __"; "Is 237 even or odd?" | 399 → 4000; judging even/odd by first digit | Thousand chart strips, base-ten blocks
6. Skip count by 3s, 4s, 6s, 25s; number patterns to 10 000 | 0–10 000 | "0, 25, 50, __"; "3 985 + 10 = ?" | Only knowing counts starting at 0 | Number line with jumps
7. Integers on a number line; negative numbers in context (temperature, elevation) | -50 to 50 | "What is 3 less than -1?"; "Which is colder: -8 or -3?" | Treating -8 as bigger than -3 because 8 > 3 | Vertical number line (thermometer)
8. Count by decimals and fractions (0.1, 0.25, 1/2) | 0–100 | "0.7, 0.8, 0.9, __"; "1/2, 1, 1 1/2, __" | 0.9 → 0.10 | Decimal number line, fraction number line
9. Large numbers to one million; count by thousands; square numbers and triangular numbers | to 1 000 000 | "999 000 + 1 000 = ?"; "Next square after 36?" | Reading 1 000 000 as "one thousand thousand" wrong, missing zeros | Place value chart (groups of three)
10. Integers and rationals: count by -3s, by 0.05s, by 1/8s; sequences with rule | -100 to 1 000 000 | "Continue: 2, -1, -4, __"; "0.85, 0.90, __" | Direction errors when stepping through zero | Number line spanning zero

### A2. Addition

1. Add within 5 with objects; "and 1 more" | sums ≤ 5 | "2 + 3"; "3 and 1 more" | Counting all three times (count 2, count 3, then recount) | Ten frame, counters
2. Add within 10; combinations of 10 | sums ≤ 10 | "4 + 6"; "7 + __ = 10" | Counting on from the wrong addend; fingers running out at 10 | Ten frame, rekenrek
3. Add within 20 with strategies: doubles, near doubles, make ten | sums ≤ 20 | "8 + 7" (make ten: 8+2+5); "6 + 6" | Counting on by ones past 10 and losing track | Two ten frames, number bonds
4. Two-digit + one-digit, and + multiples of 10, no regrouping then with | sums ≤ 100 | "34 + 5"; "47 + 20"; "38 + 6" | Adding tens to ones (47 + 20 = 49) | Hundred chart, base-ten blocks
5. Two-digit + two-digit with regrouping, mental and written | sums ≤ 200 | "67 + 58"; "25 + 25 + 25" | Forgetting the carried ten; writing 11 in the ones column | Base-ten blocks, open number line (jump strategy)
6. Three-digit addition with regrouping across hundreds; 3 addends | sums ≤ 2000 | "478 + 356"; "199 + 1" | Carrying twice; 199 + 1 → 1 100 | Base-ten blocks, place value chart
7. Four-digit and beyond with column algorithm; add money | sums ≤ 100 000 | "4 835 + 2 967"; "$12.45 + $7.80" | Misaligned columns when digit counts differ | Place value chart
8. Add decimals (tenths, hundredths) and like-denominator fractions | decimals to 2 places | "3.7 + 2.85"; "3/8 + 2/8" | Lining up decimals on the right instead of the point; adding denominators | Decimal grid (hundredths square), fraction bars
9. Add unlike-denominator fractions, mixed numbers; add integers | fractions with denominators to 12 | "2/3 + 1/4"; "-7 + 12"; "3 1/2 + 2 3/4" | Adding numerators and denominators; -7 + 12 = -19 | Fraction bars, number line through zero
10. Multi-decimal and rational addition; mental compensation with large numbers | any | "-4.5 + 2.75"; "3 999 + 2 998 (mentally)" | Sign errors; decimal alignment | Number line, place value chart

### A3. Subtraction

1. Take away within 5 | ≤ 5 | "5 take away 2" | Removing the wrong number of objects | Counters, ten frame
2. Subtract within 10; related facts to addition | ≤ 10 | "9 − 4"; "10 − __ = 3" | Counting down and including the start number ("9, 8, 7, 6" says 6 for 9 − 4) | Ten frame, number bonds (part-part-whole)
3. Subtract within 20 using think-addition, count back, make ten back | ≤ 20 | "15 − 7" (15 − 5 − 2); "13 − 6" | Seeing subtraction as unrelated to addition; always counting back by ones | Two ten frames, number line
4. Two-digit − one-digit, − multiples of 10, with and without regrouping | ≤ 100 | "45 − 3"; "70 − 40"; "42 − 7" | Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger regardless of position (42 − 7 = 45) | Hundred chart, base-ten blocks
5. Two-digit − two-digit with regrouping; find the difference (counting up) | ≤ 100 | "73 − 48"; "What is the difference between 52 and 29?" | Smaller-from-larger bug; forgetting to reduce the tens after borrowing | Base-ten blocks (trade), open number line (count up)
6. Three-digit subtraction, including across zeros | ≤ 1000 | "604 − 257"; "1000 − 365" | Zero-column errors (writing the subtrahend digit); double borrowing | Base-ten blocks, number line
7. Multi-digit subtraction; money with change | ≤ 100 000 | "5 003 − 1 478"; "$20.00 − $13.65" | Borrowing across multiple zeros | Place value chart, counting up on number line (cashier method)
8. Subtract decimals and like fractions; elapsed time as subtraction | decimals to 2 places | "6.3 − 2.85"; "5/6 − 1/6" | Not padding 6.3 to 6.30 | Hundredths grid, fraction bars
9. Subtract unlike fractions, mixed numbers with regrouping; subtract integers | denominators to 12; integers −100 to 100 | "3 1/4 − 1 3/4"; "5 − (−3)"; "−2 − 6" | 5 − (−3) = 2; subtracting the wrong way in mixed numbers | Fraction bars, number line with direction arrows
10. Rational subtraction and multi-step | any | "−3.6 − 4.25"; "12 1/2 − 7.75" | Mixing fraction/decimal forms carelessly | Number line

### A4. Multiplication

1. Equal groups and repeated addition, groups of 2 and 5 | products ≤ 20 | "3 groups of 2 = ?"; "2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 4 x __" | Counting the groups instead of the objects | Equal-groups pictures, ten frames
2. Arrays and skip counting for 2s, 5s, 10s | ≤ 50 | "A 3 by 5 array has how many?"; "5 x 4" | Confusing rows and columns count with total | Arrays
3. Facts 0, 1, 2, 5, 10; commutative property | ≤ 100 | "7 x 2"; "If 5 x 8 = 40, what is 8 x 5?" | 0 x n = n; 1 x n = 1 | Arrays, hundred chart
4. Facts 3, 4, 6 via doubling (4s = double 2s, 6s = double 3s) | ≤ 100 | "6 x 7" (= 3 x 7 doubled); "4 x 9" | Memorizing without a fallback strategy, so one miss cascades | Arrays split in half, number line jumps
5. Facts 7, 8, 9 via decomposition and compensation (9 x n = 10n − n); all facts to 10 x 10 fluent | ≤ 100 | "8 x 7" (= 8 x 5 + 8 x 2); "9 x 6" | 7 x 8 = 54 (common wrong recall); 6 x 8 vs 7 x 8 mix-ups | Area model split, distributive property arrays
6. Facts to 12 x 12; multiply by 10, 100, 1000; 2-digit x 1-digit | ≤ 1000 | "12 x 11"; "34 x 6"; "7 x 300" | "Add a zero" rule applied to decimals later; forgetting to carry in 34 x 6 | Area model (30 + 4) x 6
7. 2-digit x 2-digit, 3-digit x 1-digit; multiples and factors | ≤ 10 000 | "23 x 45"; "468 x 7" | Partial product errors (23 x 45: forgetting 20 x 40 scaling) | Area model (4 boxes), standard algorithm with place value labels
8. Multi-digit x multi-digit; multiply decimals by whole numbers | ≤ 1 000 000 | "356 x 24"; "4.5 x 3"; "0.6 x 8" | Placing the decimal by alignment rather than counting places | Area model, hundredths grid
9. Decimal x decimal; fraction x whole and fraction x fraction | any | "0.4 x 0.3"; "2/3 x 3/4"; "5 x 2/3" | 0.4 x 0.3 = 1.2; believing multiplication always makes bigger | Hundredths grid overlap, fraction area model
10. Integer multiplication; mixed numbers; exponents as repeated multiplication; mental multiplication by 25, 50, 99 | any | "−6 x 7"; "1 1/2 x 2 2/3"; "2^5"; "48 x 25" | (−)(−) sign errors; 2^5 = 10 | Sign chart, area model

### A5. Division

1. Sharing equally into 2 or 3 groups with objects | ≤ 12 | "Share 6 cookies between 2 friends" | Unequal shares; forgetting to use all items | Counters into circles
2. Sharing and grouping (how many groups of 2 in 10?); halves | ≤ 20 | "10 ÷ 2"; "How many pairs in 8?" | Confusing "share into 2" with "groups of 2" (same answer here, but different model) | Arrays, equal groups
3. Division as the inverse of multiplication, facts for 2, 5, 10 | ≤ 100 | "40 ÷ 5"; "? x 5 = 35" | Treating ÷ as commutative (5 ÷ 40) | Fact family triangles, arrays
4. Division facts to 100 (all tables); divide by 1; zero rules | ≤ 100 | "56 ÷ 7"; "0 ÷ 6"; "9 ÷ 1" | 6 ÷ 0 = 0 or 6; n ÷ 1 = 1 | Arrays, fact triangles
5. Division with remainders; 2-digit ÷ 1-digit | ≤ 100 | "23 ÷ 4 = 5 R3"; "Pencils in packs of 6, 40 pencils, how many packs?" | Dropping the remainder; writing R larger than divisor | Equal groups with leftovers, number line jumps
6. 3-digit ÷ 1-digit, long division or partial quotients; interpret the remainder | ≤ 1000 | "348 ÷ 4"; "Buses hold 40; 130 kids need how many buses?" (4, not 3 R10) | Forgetting a zero in the quotient (408 ÷ 4 = 12) | Base-ten blocks sharing, partial quotients area model
7. Divide by 10, 100, 1000; divide by multiples of 10; divisibility rules 2, 3, 5, 9, 10 | ≤ 100 000 | "4 500 ÷ 100"; "Is 741 divisible by 3?" | Removing zeros from the wrong number | Place value chart (digits shift)
8. 2-digit divisors; decimal ÷ whole number; fractions as division | ≤ 100 000 | "672 ÷ 24"; "7.2 ÷ 4"; "3 ÷ 4 = 3/4" | Estimating the quotient digit wrong; placing the decimal | Partial quotients, hundredths grid
9. Divide by decimals; divide fractions (unit fraction ÷ whole, whole ÷ unit fraction) | any | "4.8 ÷ 0.6"; "6 ÷ 1/2"; "1/3 ÷ 4" | "Division makes smaller" (6 ÷ 1/2 = 3) | Measurement model: how many halves in 6?
10. Integer division; fraction ÷ fraction; mean as division; unit rates | any | "−48 ÷ 6"; "3/4 ÷ 2/3"; "$12 for 5 kg, price per kg?" | Inverting the wrong fraction | Double number line for rates

## B. Recommended starting levels (October of the school year)

Realism check: a grade 1 child in October has just finished kindergarten; most can count to 20–30, subitize to 5, add within 5–10 with fingers, name a few shapes and coins, and tell time to the hour only if taught at home. A grade 3 child in October is fluent-ish with add/sub facts to 20, adding 2-digit with regrouping, beginning multiplication, and knows halves/quarters. A grade 4 child in October knows most multiplication facts (not all fast), 3-digit add/sub, fractions on a number line, and tenths at best.

Diagnostic rule: on first play, run a 6-question placement per domain starting at the recommended level; two misses drops one level, 6/6 bumps one level up, before regular play begins.

## C. Mastery rules

Benchmarks from adaptive apps: Khan Academy marks a skill Familiar at 70–99% on a practice set, Proficient at 100% on a set, and Mastered only after answering correctly on a later mixed-skill review. IXL's SmartScore (0–100) treats 80 as proficient, 90 as the "challenge zone", 100 as mastered, and penalizes wrong answers more as the score rises. DreamBox does not publish numeric thresholds but adapts on every answer including strategy used. The pattern across all three: (a) require accuracy on a set, not just a streak; (b) require a later, mixed-context re-test before calling it mastered; (c) weight recent answers more than old ones.

Recommended rules for this game:

**Level-up (promotion).** Promote when the child has answered the last 10 questions at the current level with at least 9 correct AND the last 5 are all correct AND median response time at that level is under the level's "fluent" threshold (suggest 6 s for fact-level questions, 20 s for multi-digit, 45 s for word problems; tune per domain). A streak alone is too noisy; a rolling window of 10 with 9/10 plus a clean last 5 is the sweet spot between Khan's 100%-of-set and IXL's 80-proficient.

**Provisional vs confirmed mastery.** On promotion, mark the old level "provisional". It becomes "confirmed" only after 3 correct answers from that level appear in interleaved review on separate days (spaced retrieval: schedule at ~1 day, ~3 days, ~7 days). If any of those review items is missed, keep it provisional and add it back to the active mix.

**Slip vs gap.** A slip is one wrong answer at a level the child has 85%+ history on; treat it as noise: show the correct answer with a one-line worked example, re-ask a parallel question (same structure, new numbers) within the next 3 questions. A gap is 2 wrong in the last 5 at the current level, or 3 wrong in the last 10, or the same misconception tag twice (generators should attach a misconception tag to each distractor, e.g. `smaller-from-larger`, `add-denominators`). On a gap: drop one level immediately for the rest of the session, show a worked example that uses the level's visual model, and do not re-promote until the promotion rule is met again from the lower level. Response time doubling versus the child's own baseline, even with correct answers, counts as half a miss (counting on fingers is a signal of not-yet-fluent).

**Worked examples after errors.** After every wrong answer, show the full solution using the level's visual model (ten frame, number line, area model, etc.) before the next question, then present a near-transfer question (same skill, different numbers) within 2–3 items. Research on worked examples shows they help most for novices; for a child at 90%+ accuracy, skip the full example and just show the answer.

**Interleaving and review mix.** For each session of ~20 questions in a domain: 60% from the current level, 20% from one level below, 10% from two levels below (or a confirmed-mastered level due for spaced review), 10% from a different domain at the child's level ("surprise" items force strategy selection, which is the mechanism Rohrer's interleaving studies credit for the ~d = 0.8 effect). Never present more than 3 identical-structure questions in a row; vary operand order and unknown position (result/change/start unknown). Review items that are missed count as gap evidence for that lower level, not for the current one.

**Fact fluency specifically.** For add/sub facts to 20 and multiplication facts to 12 x 12, track each fact individually (fact-level mastery, like a flashcard deck). A fact is "known" when answered correctly under 3 s on 3 separate days. Pick the next facts by spaced repetition (Leitner boxes with 1/3/7/14-day gaps). Introduce new facts in strategy clusters (doubles, then make ten; 2s/5s/10s, then 4s via doubling, 9s via compensation) rather than in table order.

**Session hygiene.** Cap any single domain at 25 questions per session; stop a level after 3 consecutive gaps in one sitting and switch domain (frustration guard). Start each session with 2–3 easy warm-ups from a confirmed level to prime success.

**Misconception detection in generators.** Each question generator should produce distractors keyed to the level's misconception (listed above). If the child's wrong answer matches a tagged distractor twice within 10 questions, surface the targeted worked example for that misconception rather than the generic one.
